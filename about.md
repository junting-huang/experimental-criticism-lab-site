## Mission

The Experimental Criticism Lab is a humanities research lab based in Comparative Literature at Binghamton University. It supports collaborative, project-based inquiry into how literature, media, and culture reshape the practices of reading, interpretation, translation, and criticism.

Media, information, and public life have become increasingly technical, data-driven, and opaque. In this environment, criticism matters more than ever. At a moment when universities are rethinking the place of the humanities amid rapid developments in AI and data-driven culture, the lab affirms the value of rigorous analysis, interpretation, and evaluation. Experimental criticism studies complex cultural objects while also testing the tools, models, methods, and assumptions through which those objects are now read.

Building on Binghamton’s longstanding commitment to literary theory, criticism, and contemporary thought, the lab extends that tradition toward more experimental, collaborative, and technologically engaged forms of inquiry.

The lab welcomes faculty, graduate students, undergraduates, artists, technologists, translators, and other collaborators interested in rethinking the future of criticism.

## Why a Lab?

A lab structure supports team-based research and learning by doing, especially for projects that depend on collaboration among faculty, graduate students, and undergraduates. It gives students a clear framework for participation, training, and professional development, while allowing the work of criticism to unfold collectively rather than in isolation.

## In Good Company

The lab joins a growing field of humanities labs led by scholars in and around comparative literature, including Laurent Dubreuil's Humanities Lab at Cornell, Leif Weatherby's Digital Theory Lab at NYU, Jeffrey Schnapp's metaLAB at Harvard, and Dennis Tenen's Narrative Intelligence Lab at Columbia.

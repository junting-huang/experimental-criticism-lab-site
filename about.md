The Experimental Criticism Lab is based in Comparative Literature at Binghamton University. It supports collaborative, project-based inquiry into how literature, media, and culture reshape the practices of reading, interpretation, translation, and criticism.

## Mission

As media, information, and public life become increasingly shaped—and obscured—by technology, criticism matters more than ever. The lab studies complex cultural objects while also examining the tools, models, and assumptions through which those objects are now read. Experimental criticism names this double practice: reading closely while experimenting with the forms, technologies, and collaborations that make reading possible.

Building on Binghamton’s longstanding commitment to theory, philosophy, and contemporary thought, the lab extends that tradition toward experimental and technologically engaged forms of inquiry.

## Why a Lab?

Criticism is often imagined as solitary: one reader, one text, one argument. But many of the problems facing the humanities today demand shared methods and sustained collaboration.

A lab structure makes this possible. It brings together faculty, graduate students, undergraduates, artists, technologists, and translators on projects that unfold over time, and it gives students a visible path into research—from attending events, to joining working meetings, to contributing to publications, digital projects, and public programs.

## Join Us

We welcome faculty, students, artists, technologists, translators, and anyone interested in rethinking the future of criticism. The best way to get involved is to attend an information session, join a working meeting, or reach out through the contact page.
